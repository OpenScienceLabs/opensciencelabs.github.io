# Consulting

Open Science Labs (OSL) connects organisations with independent partners that
provide professional support for open-source software, research software,
scientific computing, technical education, and related projects.

Our role is to help bring the right people together.

Rather than operating as a traditional consulting company, OSL is building a
network of consulting partners that can evaluate projects, define a scope of
work, and deliver professional services directly to clients.

If your university, research group, nonprofit, company, open-source project, or
community needs technical support, tell us what you are trying to accomplish.

We can help identify an appropriate path and, when possible, connect you with a
partner that has relevant expertise.

## How our consulting network works

### 1. Tell us what you need

Contact OSL with a short description of your project, repository, research
workflow, or technical problem.

Helpful information includes:

- the project or repository;
- what you would like to accomplish;
- important technical or scientific requirements;
- your preferred timeline;
- your approximate budget or funding constraints;
- whether the work needs to be public;
- whether confidential information or restricted systems are involved.

You do not need to prepare a complete specification before contacting us.

### 2. We explore the right route

OSL reviews the enquiry at a high level and considers whether one of our
consulting partners may be a suitable fit.

When appropriate, we can make an introduction. If an existing partner does not
match the request, we may explore other organisations or specialists in our
network.

We cannot guarantee that a suitable provider will be available for every
project.

### 3. Meet the consulting partner

The consulting partner evaluates the project with you and determines whether it
can take on the engagement.

Depending on the project, the partner may discuss:

- scope and deliverables;
- milestones and acceptance criteria;
- technical approach;
- timeline and budget;
- intellectual property;
- confidentiality;
- maintenance and support;
- other project-specific requirements.

### 4. Agree directly with the partner

Unless explicitly arranged otherwise, the consulting partner is responsible for
the professional engagement.

The client and partner establish their own contractual, financial, legal, tax,
confidentiality, intellectual-property, and delivery arrangements.

Open Science Labs is not automatically a party to agreements between a client
and an independent consulting partner.

### 5. Build something useful

The partner delivers the agreed work in collaboration with the client,
maintainers, researchers, or other stakeholders involved in the project.

OSL encourages approaches that strengthen open-source ecosystems, promote
maintainability, and contribute improvements upstream whenever practical.

## Areas our partners may support

Capabilities vary between partners and projects, but consulting engagements may
include areas such as:

### Open-source maintenance

- bug investigation and fixes;
- dependency updates;
- compatibility work;
- test improvements;
- continuous integration;
- technical debt reduction;
- issue and pull-request workflows;
- release preparation;
- maintenance planning.

### New features and integrations

- new library functionality;
- APIs and command-line interfaces;
- integrations with external tools and services;
- import and export formats;
- workflow automation;
- prototypes;
- upstream contributions.

### Packaging, releases, and distribution

- Python packaging and PyPI releases;
- conda and conda-forge;
- R packaging;
- containers;
- release automation;
- version and dependency management;
- installation testing;
- release documentation.

### Research and scientific software

- turning scripts and notebooks into reusable software;
- research software architecture;
- reproducible computational environments;
- scientific testing and validation infrastructure;
- research data pipelines;
- interoperability;
- performance optimisation;
- software citation metadata;
- archival releases;
- sustainable research software practices.

Software engineering can improve reliability, reproducibility, transparency,
and maintainability, but does not replace scientific, clinical, regulatory, or
domain-specific validation.

### Documentation and contributor experience

- user and API documentation;
- tutorials;
- developer guides;
- contribution guidelines;
- maintainer documentation;
- onboarding;
- release processes;
- architecture documentation;
- project roadmaps.

### Open-source strategy and sustainability

- upstream contribution strategy;
- project sustainability;
- maintenance planning;
- contributor workflows;
- governance;
- reducing single-maintainer dependencies;
- release and compatibility policies;
- long-term planning for grant-funded software.

### Mentoring, workshops, and training

Depending on the partner and available expertise, engagements may include:

- open-source contribution training;
- developer workshops;
- research software practices;
- technical mentoring;
- packaging and release training;
- contributor onboarding;
- training around specific open-source technologies.

These examples are not a fixed catalogue. If your need is not listed, contact
us and tell us about it.

## Our first consulting partner

### SciStitch

**SciStitch is the first consulting partner in the Open Science Labs consulting
network.**

SciStitch began as OSL's initial internal structure for professional consulting.
As the initiative evolved, we decided to move toward a decentralised partner
model: dedicated organisations can focus on professional consulting and service
delivery, while Open Science Labs continues focusing on its broader mission
around open source, open science, community, education, mentorship, and social
impact.

This structure also allows OSL to collaborate with multiple consulting
organisations over time instead of limiting the initiative to a single internal
team.

- [Visit SciStitch](https://scistitch.com/)
- [Learn about the SciStitch partnership model](https://scistitch.com/partnership/)

Services, availability, pricing, contractual terms, project acceptance, and
delivery are determined directly by SciStitch.

## Why a partner network?

Open Science Labs is a community, not a conventional software agency.

A partner-based model keeps that distinction clear while still helping
organisations find professional support.

It also gives OSL the flexibility to build relationships with organisations and
specialists with different areas of expertise.

Over time, we hope this can create a broader ecosystem connecting:

**organisations that need help → professional service providers → open-source
projects → maintainers and contributors**

This can create value across the ecosystem:

- organisations gain access to specialised expertise;
- consulting partners find meaningful projects;
- open-source projects receive professional engineering and maintenance;
- maintainers and contributors may gain new opportunities to collaborate and
  grow professionally.

Participation in paid work is never automatic and depends on the consulting
partner, project requirements, funding, contributor readiness, and the
arrangements established for each project.

## Our principles

### Open whenever practical

We encourage upstream contributions, open standards, reusable solutions,
documentation, and public technical knowledge whenever the project allows it.

### Respect project communities

Existing maintainers, governance structures, contribution processes, and
community decisions should be respected.

### Build for maintainability

Professional delivery should consider what happens after the initial task is
completed. Tests, documentation, packaging, release processes, and handover can
be as important as the feature itself.

### Support people as well as software

Open source is sustained by people. We are particularly interested in
relationships that can strengthen maintainers, researchers, contributors,
communities, and the long-term sustainability of the projects involved.

### Keep relationships clear

OSL, consulting partners, clients, and community programmes have different
roles.

Professional consulting should not depend on unpaid volunteers or create
commercial delivery obligations for participants in OSL internships, mentorship
programmes, or community activities.

## Looking for something different?

Our current partners may not cover every technology, scientific discipline,
country, or type of engagement.

That should not stop you from contacting us.

If you need a different type of consulting or professional support, we may be
able to:

- identify another potential partner;
- connect you with an affiliated project or specialist;
- explore collaboration with another organisation;
- introduce you to someone in the wider open-source or research software
  ecosystem;
- suggest a different route when OSL is not the appropriate place for the
  request.

An introduction from OSL does not guarantee that a provider will accept the
project, and clients remain responsible for evaluating whether an independent
partner is appropriate for their needs.

## Become a consulting partner

We would like this initiative to grow beyond a single organisation.

Companies, cooperatives, research software groups, independent specialists,
nonprofits, and other organisations with professional expertise in open source,
science, research software, education, or related technical areas are welcome to
discuss becoming part of the OSL consulting network.

We are particularly interested in partners that:

- understand open-source communities;
- respect upstream projects and their maintainers;
- value transparent and maintainable engineering;
- are comfortable collaborating across organisations;
- support open science, education, research, or social-impact work;
- can provide professional services through an appropriate independent legal
  and financial structure.

Partnership does not mean that OSL guarantees projects, revenue, clients, or
exclusive access to opportunities.

Our goal is to build a network in which organisations can discover appropriate
expertise and collaborators more easily.

[Contact Open Science Labs about becoming a consulting partner](mailto:team@opensciencelabs.org?subject=OSL%20consulting%20partnership)

## For the OSL community

This initiative is also part of a broader goal: creating more pathways between
community participation and professional open-source work.

Consulting engagements are managed by the partner delivering the service,
including decisions about staffing, compensation, contracting, and contributor
participation.

Where appropriate, a partner may decide to collaborate with contributors from
the OSL ecosystem.

However:

- community participation does not create an entitlement to paid consulting
  work;
- internships and volunteer programmes are separate from commercial
  engagements;
- contributors should not be expected to fulfil client obligations without an
  appropriate professional arrangement;
- each consulting partner determines how it selects and works with
  contributors.

We hope that, as the partner network grows, it can create more opportunities for
people to gain experience, collaborate across organisations, and build
sustainable careers around open source and research software.
